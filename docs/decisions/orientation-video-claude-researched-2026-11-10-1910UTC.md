# Orientation video: decision log

Made 2026-11-10 in the thread "Make 3b1b-style orientation video". The person gave full authority to decide; each choice below lists what else was on the table and why this won.

| # | Decision | Alternatives considered | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Length 4:44** (hard cap was 5:00, ask was 3 to 5) | A 3-minute cut covering only alternatives and Ghost | The person said new users are "at a loss" on the first screen. Four ideas plus "your first minute" plus the honest today-vs-planned section needed about 680 words. The first draft ran 4:57; trimming lines and dropping the spoken credits (credits stay on screen) brought it to 4:44, leaving room under the cap. |
| 2 | **Grant's ManimGL (3b1b/manim), not Manim Community** | ManimCE, which the earlier Vaani Sakhi pipeline used | The person asked for Grant's library by name. It now renders through wgpu; headless it works on Mesa's lavapipe Vulkan driver under xvfb, so no GPU was needed. |
| 3 | **Structure borrowed from Grant's neural-network video** (3b1b/videos, `_2017/nn/part1.py`, scenes `ExampleThrees` → `LayOutPlan` → `BreakDownName` …) | A feature tour in UI order | That video opens on one concrete, slightly messy example (a sloppy 3), states the challenge, lays out the plan, then takes one idea at a time and is candid about limits. Ours: one sentence that "feels off" → keep or delete → "keep both" → four numbered ideas → limits (what is not built yet). |
| 4 | **Original visuals only, in the app's own look** (navy page, IBM Plex Mono, the app's exact colour tokens from `src/ui/styles.css`) | Screen recordings of the app; Grant's Pi creatures | No footage or frames from Jason Fried's video, as the person requires. Pi creatures are 3b1b's characters, so not reused. Matching the app's palette means the video teaches the same colour vocabulary the editor uses (violet word, rose sentence, copper paragraph; green kept, amber undecided, red rejected; electric blue only for "planned"). |

| 6 | **"A dial, not keep-or-delete"** as the central metaphor, plus "a draft is a tree of possibilities" | Version-control or git metaphors | The master spec's thesis is "alternatives, not versions". ## Known limits of this cut

- Narration was checked by timing and loudness, not by a human ear; mispronunciations, if any, are fixed by adding a respelling to `script/lexicon.yaml` and re-running only the changed beats.
- The "?" button and welcome card come from the app thread's PR #19; if that UI changes, scene 5 (beat 5.5) should change with it.

| 19 | **Cuts snap to pauses, then every cut is checked by ear-proxy** (Gemini transcription of the supercut, asking only for clipped words or stray fragments), repeated until none were reported | Cutting on word timestamps alone | Word timestamps from transcription were off by 0.1 to 0.5 s; the first pass clipped six words. Each edge now moves to the nearest pause (an energy envelope, 13 dB under the median speech level for 60 ms), and edges the check flagged are set by hand from the envelope. |
| 20 | **One room for two voices**: the recording runs through a voice chain (high-pass 90 Hz, FFT denoise, a low-mid dip, a presence lift, gentle compression, a soft expander in pauses); the synthesized narration gets a short early reflection and a softer top; a quiet room-tone bed made from the recording's own pauses runs under the whole video; both parts are set by linear gain to the same loudness (intro -16.6 LUFS, narration -16.5 LUFS, peaks under -1.3 dBFS) | Dynamic loudnorm on the recording; leaving the narration dry | The author asked for the two to sound like "one, combined, golden whole". Dynamic loudnorm lifted the noise between words, so linear gain won. A recorded laptop mic and a studio TTS voice can't be made identical; sharing the room tone and loudness removes the jump at the handoff, which is what the ear notices most. |
| 21 | **On-screen cards for each fact while the author speaks**, plus a live trace of their voice and "a note from Ashish, who built write-on / in their own voice" | Holding one still frame; a waveform only | The cards let a viewer with the sound off follow the intro, and spell names and handles the ear can miss (@poteto, pstack, Perell, Celine Nguyen). The handle the author pronounced "potato" is written as @poteto, the author of the unslop skill. Captions skip "uh" and "um". |
| 22 | **The recording stays out of this repo**; `tools/supercut.py` reads it from `$VOICE_DIR` | Committing the audio | The author's rule: voice recordings never go into the public repo. Only clip times, captions and a per-frame loudness envelope (used to draw the trace) are committed. |

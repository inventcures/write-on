# Orientation video: decision log

Made 2026-11-10 in the thread "Make 3b1b-style orientation video". The person gave full authority to decide; each choice below lists what else was on the table and why this won.

| # | Decision | Alternatives considered | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Length 4:44** (hard cap was 5:00, ask was 3 to 5) | A 3-minute cut covering only alternatives and Ghost | The person said new users are "at a loss" on the first screen. Four ideas plus "your first minute" plus the honest today-vs-planned section needed about 680 words. The first draft ran 4:57; trimming lines and dropping the spoken credits (credits stay on screen) brought it to 4:44, leaving room under the cap. |
| 2 | **Grant's ManimGL (3b1b/manim), not Manim Community** | ManimCE, which the earlier Vaani Sakhi pipeline used | The person asked for Grant's library by name. It now renders through wgpu; headless it works on Mesa's lavapipe Vulkan driver under xvfb, so no GPU was needed. |
| 3 | **Structure borrowed from Grant's neural-network video** (3b1b/videos, `_2017/nn/part1.py`, scenes `ExampleThrees` → `LayOutPlan` → `BreakDownName` …) | A feature tour in UI order | That video opens on one concrete, slightly messy example (a sloppy 3), states the challenge, lays out the plan, then takes one idea at a time and is candid about limits. Ours: one sentence that "feels off" → keep or delete → "keep both" → four numbered ideas → limits (what is not built yet). |
| 4 | **Original visuals only, in the app's own look** (navy page, IBM Plex Mono, the app's exact colour tokens from `src/ui/styles.css`) | Screen recordings of the app; Grant's Pi creatures | No footage or frames from Jason Fried's video, as the person requires. Pi creatures are 3b1b's characters, so not reused. Matching the app's palette means the video teaches the same colour vocabulary the editor uses (violet word, rose sentence, copper paragraph; green kept, amber undecided, red rejected; electric blue only for "planned"). |
| 5 | **Teach habits, not features** ("torn → add a variant; unsure → ghost; love it but it doesn't fit → Overflow") and a "first minute" segment | A full shortcut list | The person's message: the tool adapts to the writer, but people with set habits need a basic orientation. Habits map a feeling the writer already has (the urge to delete) onto a tool. The first-minute segment answers the screenshot: the app opens in read mode, Ctrl+Shift+R brings the tools in, the "?" button brings the video back. |
| 6 | **"A dial, not keep-or-delete"** as the central metaphor, plus "a draft is a tree of possibilities" | Version-control or git metaphors | The master spec's thesis is "alternatives, not versions". A dial (keep, variant, ghost, Overflow, delete) shows the four soft moves before the hard one; the tree shows why holding branches open is the point. |
| 7 | **Honest about Phase 0** with a solid TODAY box and a dashed PLANNED box | Leaving the AI plans out, or showing them as features | The coordinator asked for honesty; the spec says Phase 0 has no AI (ADR-0005). Planned items (learning from choices and past writing, nudges as questions, editor personas) are dashed and in machine blue, the app's colour for machine-made things. The "8 of 10 shorter" example is the spec's own Bradley-Terry example, shown as a pattern, not as a working feature. |
| 8 | **Narration: Gemini TTS, voice Sulafat**, same style prompt family and -16 LUFS loudness as the person's earlier explainers | Cartesia (the fallback in their .env) | The person asked for the same voice as their MIT Solve pitch. The model name in their config, `gemini-3.8-flash-tts`, still works but only with google-genai 2.30.1 (the 3.0 SDK rejects the call), so the SDK is pinned. Tempo 1.16, as in their pitch. 34 beats were synthesized once and cached; no beat was paid for twice. |
| 9 | **Spoken respellings** (`write-on` → "Write On", `Ctrl+/` → "Control slash") only in the TTS text | Writing them out in the captions too | Captions should show what the user presses; only the voice needs the spelling-out. |
| 10 | **1080p, 30 fps, H.264 + AAC, 11 MB**, with WebVTT captions and a poster frame | 60 fps (Grant's default) or 4K | The app thread asked for a committable file under 40 MB. Mostly-static text animation loses nothing at 30 fps. |
| 11 | **Hosting: the app serves the file as `/explainer.mp4`** (with `/explainer.vtt` and `/explainer.jpg`), committed by the app thread; the project page embeds that URL | YouTube; a GitHub release; a copy in the homepage repo | One copy, served with byte ranges by our own server, no third-party tracking, and the app thread already built the slot (PR #19). The project page links to the same file, so an updated video updates both. |
| 12 | **The video's source lives in `explainer/` of this repo, without the rendered video, audio or keys** | Keeping it only on the laptop | Anyone can re-render after the app changes; only the TTS step needs a key, read from `$WO_ENV_FILE`. |
| 13 | **Credits on screen at the end**: Jason Fried's Write_On as the inspiration, Grant Sanderson's manim, Gemini TTS | Spoken credits | Saves 6 seconds of narration; the person's standing rule is full credit to Fried. |

## Known limits of this cut

- Narration was checked by timing and loudness, not by a human ear; mispronunciations, if any, are fixed by adding a respelling to `script/lexicon.yaml` and re-running only the changed beats.
- The "?" button and welcome card come from the app thread's PR #19; if that UI changes, scene 5 (beat 5.5) should change with it.
